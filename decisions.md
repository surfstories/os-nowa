# Decisions

Append-only. Newest at the bottom. **Never edit or delete an entry.**

This is the most valuable file in the system and it is the one that looks least useful on day one.
Its job is to answer the question you will actually ask in six months, which is never *"what did I
decide?"* — it is **_"why did I decide that, and what did I already rule out?"_** Without the
reasoning, you re-litigate the same choice every few months and sometimes reverse it by accident.

## The entry shape

```markdown
## 2026-08-15 — Monthly plan rather than annual

Paying monthly even though annual is 15% cheaper.

**Why:** the cash-flow certainty is worth more right now than the discount, and the annual
commitment locks in a tool we have used for three weeks.

**Rejected:** annual billing — revisit once we have used it for a full quarter.
```

Three parts, and the second two are what make it worth writing:

- **What was decided**, in one sentence.
- **Why** — the reasoning that would let a stranger, or you in six months, judge whether it still
  holds.
- **Rejected** — what the alternative was, and what would make it right after all. This is the part
  people skip and the part that pays.

## Reversing a decision

Write a **new** entry that says so and names the entry it supersedes. The old one stays exactly as
it was.

A reversed decision is not an embarrassment to be tidied away — it is the most informative thing in
this file. It records that the reasoning was tested against reality and lost, which is the one thing
you cannot reconstruct later.
