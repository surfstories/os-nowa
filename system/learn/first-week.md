# Your first week

Setup is done. Here is what actually makes this stick — and what to ignore for now.

## The only habit that matters

**Work from this folder.** Open a conversation here rather than a blank one somewhere else.

That is the whole thing. Everything else in this system is machinery for making that habit pay off:
because you worked here, the agent already knows who you are, and what you did last Tuesday is
written down instead of gone.

A conversation somewhere else is a conversation this system learns nothing from.

## Days one to three: just use it

Do not organise anything. Do not add domains. Bring it real work — a document to make sense of, a
message to draft, something to think through.

Two things to try when they come up naturally:

- **When something gets decided, say so.** "Log that." Three sentences in `decisions.md` now saves an
  hour of re-deciding in March.
- **When you catch yourself explaining something twice**, that is the signal something should be
  written down. Ask for it to be.

## Day four or so: notice what has piled up

Ask: *"what have we got so far?"*

If material about one subject is scattered, that is when a domain earns its place —
`system/learn/adding-a-domain.md`. Not before. A folder structure designed on day one is a guess; one
that grows out of a week of real use fits.

## End of the first week: two things worth saying

- **"level up"** — finds one thing you did by hand three or more times this week and turns it into
  one thing you never do again. Once a week is plenty.
- **"check my system"** — a health check. Worth running every few weeks, not weekly. Nothing decays
  in seven days.

## What to ignore for now

The tier model, the catalog format, the log vocabulary. All of it is real and all of it is doing
something, but none of it needs your attention — the agent maintains it.

Ask *"how does this work?"* whenever you are curious. Curiosity is a much better time to learn a
system than day one.

## The one thing that will go wrong

You will do a big piece of work somewhere else — a different chat, a different tool — and it will not
be here.

Nothing breaks. But that work does not compound, and the version of this folder that is genuinely
useful in six months is the one that saw the real work. When you notice it happened, paste the
outcome in and ask for it to be filed. That takes a minute and closes the gap.
