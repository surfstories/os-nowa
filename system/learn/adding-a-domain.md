# Adding a new area

A **domain** is one subject you keep material on: a job, a property, a health thing, a side project,
a course. It is a folder with a catalog. That is all it is.

## When it earns a folder

You are about to add the **third** thing about a subject that has no home. Two loose files are fine.
Three is a subject.

**When in doubt, do not add one.** The common mistake is not too few domains — it is two thin ones
that should have been a single fuller one. Material about one subject split across two catalogs means
neither is complete, and you will search the wrong one.

## How to add it

Say what it should be called. A plain word beats a clever one: `flat`, `studies`, `clients`,
`health`. Then the agent will:

1. Make the folder, with an `index.md` and a `log.md` from the templates.
2. Ask what goes in it *right now* — and write that in as the first page. A domain created empty
   tends to stay empty.
3. Add the catalog row and the log line, in the same run.

Step 3 is not bookkeeping. A page with no catalog row is invisible to every future conversation: the
work was done, the file is on disk, and nothing will ever point at it again. The full rule is in
`system/conventions.md` under "The spine rule".

## What goes where inside it

- **`sources/`** — things that came from outside. A contract, a document someone sent, an export.
  Read them, never edit them: they are the evidence, and once edited they cannot settle an argument.
- **`pages/`** — what you and the agent wrote. Notes, summaries, what was worked out. These get
  updated freely.

A small domain can skip both and keep files flat. It always keeps `index.md` and `log.md`.

## Renaming or splitting one later

Both are fine and both are normal — a subject that grew is a good problem.

Ask for it, and expect the agent to update the catalog rows and leave a `fix` line in the log saying
what moved. What you should *not* do is move files by hand outside a conversation: the folder will be
right and every pointer to it will be wrong, and nothing will tell you.
